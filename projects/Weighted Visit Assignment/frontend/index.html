      Evidence

      Upload a calendar

      Print the Outlook calendar to PDF and drop it here. Nothing to set up: the file names its own calendars and the board syncs on upload. Work Week or Day view gives real start and end times it can schedule against. A Month view shows who has room on which days, but prints no end times, so it never claims anyone is free at a particular hour.

      From the all-day banners

      Who cannot take visits

      The lab posts whole-day notices like “Ramiro unavailable for visits”. Those days are blocked outright. This is the only event text the board reads: a notice has to match that exact shape, and anything else on the page — ordinary meeting subjects included — is discarded rather than stored.

      Lab policy

      Scheduling filters

      These come from the lab's own calendars, not from anyone's diary. Two of them say when a visit may happen; one says when the lab room is already taken. A filter with no entries in the uploaded range is shown as off rather than hidden — an empty calendar and a missing one mean different things.

      From the upload

      Who is free this month

      Read straight off the calendar you uploaded. A month print shows how many commitments each person has on each day, not the gaps between them — so this says who has room, not what time to book.

      Fallback

      Match colours to people

      This export's header lost its calendar colours, so the board could not work out who is who on its own. Open the shared calendar in Outlook, read off who owns each colour, and record it once here. Until that is done, entries are counted but belong to nobody.
